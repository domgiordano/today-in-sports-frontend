Documentation

How the questions are made, how they are scored, and where every fact comes from.

What it is

A five-question quiz, the same for everyone, refreshed every day at midnight UTC. Each question is anchored to today's calendar date — something that actually happened on this day in a previous year.

The questions walk backwards through time. The first is from the last twelve months; the last reaches thirty years back or further.

Where questions come from

Every game ever played in six sports is ingested from public archives — 235,512 so far. Nothing is scraped from articles, and no model is asked what happened. A question is assembled from fields in a specific row: the teams, the score, the date, the pitcher of record.

Because of that, every question can name its source, and the review tool shows that link beside the answer. A question that cannot cite a record is rejected before anyone sees it.

What counts as notable

Most games are not worth asking about. Significance is decided by rule rather than opinion — a no-hitter is a game where a team recorded zero hits; a league title is clinched on the day the lead becomes mathematically uncatchable; a career milestone is a running total crossing a round number.

Rules are calibrated by how often they fire. Anything triggering more than roughly fifteen times a season is measuring something that merely happens, not something remarkable, and is tightened or dropped. A plain 1-0 baseball game occurs about 59 times a season, so only 1-0 games that also go to extra innings qualify.

Scoring

Accuracy is what matters. Each question has a base value that rises with how far back it reaches:

Answering quickly adds a bonus, but a capped one — at most a quarter of the question's base value, and it never falls to zero however long you take. A fast wrong answer can never beat a slow right one. The game is about knowing sport, not about reflexes.

Questions asking for a number award partial credit on how close you get, so a near miss is worth something and a wild guess is not.

Keeping it fair

Everyone plays the identical quiz, so the answers are never sent to your browser before you commit. Questions arrive one at a time, your answer is graded on the server, and the clock is started server-side when the question is handed over — not by your device.

One attempt per day. Refreshing mid-quiz resumes where you left off rather than restarting or granting a second run.

Questions

Why is the last question so hard?

It is meant to be. But the deep questions are built to be worked out rather than remembered — ordering events, estimating a number, reasoning from what you do know. You should not need to have watched a game in 1965 to have a chance at it.

Do I need an account?

To play, no. To appear on a leaderboard, yes — otherwise scores could be replayed endlessly by clearing your browser.

A question looks wrong. What now?

Every question links to the record it came from, so it can be checked directly. Where the archive itself is thin or ambiguous, the question is supposed to be rejected rather than published — and if one slips through, that is a bug worth reporting.
